# 0003 — An invitation is accepted by the database, for the address it was sent to

**Date:** 2026-09-24 · **Status:** accepted · amends ADR 0002

## Context

The security audit of 2026-09-24 read the invitation flow against the barrier
ADR 0002 put under it, and found three things, each reproduced against
`planora_app`.

**Accepting did not work.** `acceptInvitation` inserted the membership and then
marked the invitation accepted with an `UPDATE` on the invitation lane. The
policy on `workspace_invitations` admitted the lane to *read* the row by its
hash, but its `WITH CHECK` binds every write to `app.current_workspace()`, which
on that lane is nobody's: the update was refused with 42501 and the whole
acceptance rolled back. The barrier went on in production on 2026-09-22, so
from that day every "Entrar no espaço" failed. Nothing noticed because the
barrier's own test inserted a membership by hand and never called the service,
and every other suite and the E2E server connect as the owner.

**An invitation could make an owner.** `inviteMemberAction` accepted any of the
five roles, `prepareInvitation` checked only that the inviter could manage
members, and acceptance copied the role across. An admin — who §4.2.1 says may
not delete the workspace — could invite a second account of their own as
`owner` and delete it from there. The form never offered `owner`; the Server
Action is a public endpoint and does not care what the form offers.

**An invitation was a bearer token.** Acceptance matched the token's hash and
nothing else. The invitation's `email` column was stored and never read, so a
forwarded, logged or shared link let any signed-in account join with the
invited role. Two accounts accepting the same link at once could both get in:
the read and the update were separate statements with no lock between them.

The obvious repair to the first — widening the `WITH CHECK` so the invitation
lane may update its invitation — would have let that lane rewrite any column of
the row, `role` included. The policy on `workspace_members` already let the
lane insert a membership with any role it liked, and update its own.

## Decision

**Joining is one function, and the lane only reads.** `app.accept_invitation(membership_id)`
is `SECURITY DEFINER`, owned by the owner, `search_path` pinned with `pg_temp`
last and `EXECUTE` granted to `planora_app` alone. It reads the lane's two
settings — the token's hash and the person — and does the whole acceptance in
one statement: it locks the invitation row, decides, marks it accepted and
writes the membership with the role *the invitation carries*. It answers with
an outcome (`joined`, `invalid-token`, `expired`, `wrong-account`,
`already-member`), not an exception, because each of those is a refusal the page
has words for. The membership's id is passed in because ids are generated by
the application (§4.1), not by the database.

The policies shrink to match. `workspace_members` loses the invitation branch
of its `WITH CHECK`: no lane may insert a membership into a workspace it is not
already in, and the only way in from outside is the function. The invitation
lane keeps a `SELECT`-only policy on `workspace_invitations`, so the page can
still say "expired" or "already used", and no longer has any write at all.

**The invitation belongs to its address.** The function refuses unless the
person presenting the token has a verified account whose address is the one the
invitation was sent to. The page checks the same thing before it offers the
button, and says so in words rather than failing on the click. Somebody new
registers with the address the invitation reached, which is the address the
link arrived at.

**Nobody grants more than they hold, and nobody grants ownership.**
`grantableRoles(context)` beside `can()` is the rule: the roles below `owner`
that rank no higher than the inviter's own — so an owner or an admin may invite
an admin, a manager, a member or a viewer, and nobody else may invite at all.
The service refuses anything else as `forbidden-role`, and the members screen
offers exactly that list, computed on the server. The database refuses an
`owner` invitation at acceptance whatever the service did, so an owner
invitation written before this change cannot be redeemed.

**The claim is atomic.** The function takes the invitation row `FOR UPDATE`, so
a second acceptance racing the first waits, re-reads the row as accepted and is
told the invitation does not exist any more.

## Consequences

- Invitations work again with the barrier on, and the barrier test now joins a
  workspace through the service, as `planora_app`, the way the page does.
- The invitation lane can no longer write anything. The page reads through it;
  the click goes through the function.
- Ownership is not transferable by invitation. When a transfer is wanted it
  will be its own action, owner-only, and its own decision.
- An admin can make another admin. §4.2.1 gives admins the members column, and
  granting a role one holds is not an escalation.
- A pending invitation with role `owner` written before this change stays in the
  table and can no longer be redeemed; the page shows it as not existing. It is
  not deleted by the migration, so whoever operates the deployment can see
  whether one was ever issued (`docs/DEPLOY.md` §7.5).
- Somebody who signs in with a different account than the one invited is told
  which is wrong and not which address is right: whoever holds a forwarded link
  learns nothing new from it.

See ADR 0002 for the lanes, and `docs/DEVELOPMENT_PLAN.md` §2.4 and §4.2.1.
